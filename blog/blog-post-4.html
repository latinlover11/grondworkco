Designing the Perfect Outdoor Kitchen for Entertaining

December 5, 2023 9 min read

An outdoor kitchen transforms your backyard into a true extension of your living space, creating a natural flow between indoor and outdoor entertaining. In Colorado, where we cherish our short but glorious outdoor season, a well-designed outdoor kitchen allows you to maximize every pleasant day and evening.

However, designing an outdoor kitchen that's both beautiful and functional requires careful consideration of our unique climate, your cooking style, and how you like to entertain. After designing and building numerous outdoor kitchens throughout Longmont and Boulder County, we've developed a comprehensive approach that balances aesthetics, durability, and practicality. Here's our guide to creating the perfect outdoor cooking space for your Colorado home:

1. Start with Function: How Will You Actually Use It?

Before selecting materials or appliances, honestly assess how you plan to use your outdoor kitchen:

  • Cooking style: Are you a gourmet chef who needs multiple burners, a griddle, and a pizza oven? Or do you prefer simple grilling for weeknight meals?
  • Entertainment frequency: Will this be used daily for family meals, weekly for gatherings, or occasionally for large parties?
  • Menu preferences: Do you focus on grilled meats, vegetarian options, wood-fired pizza, or a variety of cuisines?
  • Prep vs. cooking: Do you prefer to do most prep indoors and just cook outside, or do you want full prep capabilities outdoors?

Your answers will determine the layout, appliance selection, and workflow efficiency of your outdoor kitchen.

2. The Work Triangle: Applying Kitchen Design Principles Outdoors

Just like indoor kitchens, outdoor kitchens benefit from the classic "work triangle" concept:

  • The three points: Refrigeration, cooking surface, and sink/cleanup area
  • Optimal distances: Each leg of the triangle should be between 4-9 feet, with the total perimeter not exceeding 26 feet
  • Workflow efficiency: This layout minimizes steps and creates a natural cooking rhythm
  • Zoning: Consider separate zones for prep, cooking, and serving/cleanup

In outdoor kitchens, we often adapt this principle to account for grills, smokers, pizza ovens, and beverage centers.

3. Climate-Specific Considerations for Colorado

Our semi-arid climate with intense sun, temperature swings, and occasional moisture requires special attention:

  • Material selection: Choose UV-stable, frost-resistant materials that won't crack, fade, or deteriorate
  • Wind protection: Consider prevailing wind patterns when placing grills and open flames
  • Shade solutions: Incorporate pergolas, awnings, or strategic plantings for summer comfort
  • Drainage: Ensure proper slope and drainage to handle snowmelt and summer thunderstorms
  • Winterizing: Plan for easy winterization to protect plumbing and appliances

These considerations significantly impact the longevity and usability of your outdoor kitchen.

4. Appliance Selection: Quality and Suitability

Invest in appliances specifically designed for outdoor use:

  • Grills: Look for stainless steel construction, adequate BTU output, and features like infrared searing burners
  • Refrigeration: Use outdoor-rated refrigerators and ice makers with proper ventilation
  • Sink and plumbing: Choose commercial-grade stainless steel sinks with adequate hot/cold water supply
  • Storage: Opt for weatherproof cabinets and drawers designed for outdoor temperature extremes
  • Specialty appliances: Consider pizza ovens, smokers, griddles, or beverage centers based on your cooking preferences

While outdoor appliances represent a significant investment, quality units designed for exterior use will last far longer than indoor appliances used outside.

5. Material Selection: Beauty That Lasts

Choose materials that withstand our climate while providing the aesthetic you desire:

  • Countertops: Options include granite, quartzite, concrete, and outdoor-rated tile
  • Cabinetry: Marine-grade polymer, stainless steel, or teak woods designed for exterior use
  • Flooring: Slip-resistant options like textured concrete, natural stone, or outdoor porcelain tile
  • Backsplashes: Consider stainless steel, stone, or tile that's easy to clean and heat-resistant
  • Structural elements: Use powder-coated steel, aluminum, or naturally rot-resistant woods for pergolas and supports

We particularly recommend materials with low thermal conductivity to prevent surfaces from becoming uncomfortably hot in direct sunlight.

6. Storage and Workspace: The Often-Overlooked Essentials

Adequate storage and prep space make the difference between a frustrating and joyful cooking experience:

  • Utensil storage: Drawers for tools, spices, and cooking essentials
  • Food storage: Refrigeration for perishables and dry storage for non-perishables
  • Trash and recycling: Conveniently located, bear-resistant options if needed
  • Prep space: Adequate counterspace for food preparation, plating, and serving
  • Seating: Consider bar-height seating for casual dining and socializing while cooking

Think about your specific cooking habits and ensure you have the right storage solutions for your tools, ingredients, and entertaining needs.

7. Integration with Your Outdoor Living Space

Your outdoor kitchen should feel like a natural part of your overall landscape design:

  • Flow: Create clear pathways between cooking, dining, and lounging areas
  • Visual connections: Ensure sightlines from the kitchen to dining and conversation areas
  • Lighting: Incorporate task lighting for cooking, ambient lighting for dining, and accent lighting for features
  • Utilities: Plan for adequate gas, electrical, water, and drainage connections
  • Wind and sun: Consider microclimates within your yard for optimal comfort

The best outdoor kitchens feel like they've always belonged in your landscape, not like an afterthought appliance installation.

8. Budgeting and Phasing Your Project

Outdoor kitchens can range from modest to elaborate. Consider these approaches:

  • Start with essentials: Begin with a grill, prep space, and basic storage, then add features over time
  • Prioritize based on use: Invest most in the elements you'll use most frequently
  • Consider resale value: High-quality outdoor kitchens can significantly enhance property value
  • Phased implementation: Plan the full design upfront but implement in stages as budget allows

A well-planned outdoor kitchen provides years of enjoyment and can become the heart of your home's outdoor living experience.
