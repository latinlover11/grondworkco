Spring Landscape Cleanup Checklist: Preparing Your Property for Growing Season

February 28, 2024 6 min read

Spring in Colorado brings warmer days, melting snowpack, and the exciting renewal of our outdoor living spaces. However, winter storms, high winds, and heavy snowfall leave behind accumulated leaves, dead foliage, fallen branches, and compacted soil. A thorough spring landscape cleanup is essential to set your yard up for vibrant, healthy growth throughout the upcoming season.

Whether you manage a residential property in Longmont, Boulder, or surrounding areas, follow this comprehensive spring landscape cleanup checklist to prepare your lawn, gardens, and hardscapes for peak summer beauty.

1. Clear Winter Debris and Leaf Litter

Start by clearing away leaves, pine needles, twigs, and trash that gathered over the winter months. Heavy layers of wet leaves trap moisture against turfgrass and crown plants, blocking sunlight and promoting fungal diseases like snow mold.

  • Rake remaining winter leaves out of garden beds and lawn corners.
  • Clear debris out of window wells, drains, and along fence lines.
  • Compost clean organic leaf litter or dispose of it through local green-waste recycling.

2. Prune and Cut Back Perennials & Ornamental Grasses

Early spring—before new growth pushes through—is the ideal window to cut back dormant plants and prune winter-damaged stems:

  • Ornamental Grasses: Cut back warm-season grasses (like Karl Foerster and Maiden Grass) to 2–4 inches above the ground using sharp shears.
  • Perennials: Prune dead stems from lavender, Russian sage, penstemon, and cone-flowers to encourage sturdy new shoots.
  • Deciduous Trees & Shrubs: Remove broken, diseased, or crossing branches to improve airflow and plant architecture.

3. Redefine Garden Bed Edges & Top-Dress Mulch

Crisp garden bed edges instantly elevate property curb appeal and keep turfgrass roots from invading plant beds. Spade a clean 3-to-4-inch deep vertical trench along all bed borders, then apply a fresh 2-inch top-dressing of organic shredded bark mulch. Mulch retains soil moisture during dry spring stretches and smothers germinating weed seeds.

4. Aerate, Fertilize, and Overseed Lawn Areas

Colorado's clay soils become heavily compacted over winter. Core aeration extracts small soil plugs to allow air, water, and nutrients to penetrate deep into the root zone.

  • Core Aeration: Perform core aeration in spring or fall to relieve compaction.
  • Overseeding: Spread drought-tolerant cool-season turfgrass seed over thin or bare patches.
  • Balanced Fertilization: Apply a slow-release nitrogen fertilizer to encourage root establishment without forcing excessive top-growth.

5. Inspect Irrigation Lines & Hardscape Surfaces

Before turning on your sprinkler system for the season, conduct a thorough inspection. Inspect backflow preventers, check for broken sprinkler heads, and adjust spray patterns away from stone walls and fences. Power-wash flagstone patios and paver walkways to clear winter grime and sand.
