Sprinkler Blowout 101: Why Longmont Homes Need It Before the First Freeze

October 23, 2026 5 min read

Every October there's a predictable round of this: a hard freeze shows up on the forecast, somebody's sprinkler system still has water in it, and by the second cold night the backflow preventer above their valve box has split like a cheap garden hose. Water inside a lawn sprinkler system doesn't forgive - freezing water expands, PVC and brass doesn't, and the weakest fitting in the line becomes the damage report.

A sprinkler blowout is the fix, and it's genuinely simple: a high-volume air compressor is connected to the system, and air is pushed through every zone until the residual water is gone from the mainline, the laterals, and - critically - the backflow preventer that sits above grade. Twenty minutes of compressed air, done right, is what stands between you and a spring plumbing bill.

What actually breaks if you skip it

  • The backflow preventer. This is the expensive one, because it sits above ground where the cold hits first. A split double-check or RP assembly is a plumber visit plus parts, not a hardware-store fix.
  • Valve bodies and manifolds. Buried shallow enough to freeze, sitting wet enough to crack. Symptoms show up in April when a zone never shuts off.
  • Lateral lines and fittings. Less common than the above-ground failures, but a hairline crack underground announces itself as a soggy spot that won't dry out.

When to do it in Boulder County

Front Range rule of thumb: the first hard freeze typically lands by mid-October, and some years it comes early. The right time is when forecast lows start touching freezing - not the morning after. And if the freeze already happened? The system still has a story to tell: some years nothing splits, some years it already has. A proper check beats an assumption either way.

Why people skip it, and shouldn't

The honest reasons: it sounds technical, the compressor trips people out, and it's easy to believe the shut-off valve is enough. It isn't - the backflow preventer usually sits after the shut-off, above ground, full of water with nowhere to go. That's precisely the fitting a blowout exists to protect.
