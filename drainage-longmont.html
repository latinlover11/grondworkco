Water is patient. Grade wins eventually.

An inch of rain on a thousand square feet of roof is better than six hundred gallons, delivered in a few minutes through one downspout. Point that at a concrete slab and the slab becomes the road. Point it at mulch and the mulch ends up downstream, in the bed, on the walk, or against your foundation.

Colorado makes it worse: freeze-thaw opens seams, soil that stays saturated pushes and settles, and the low corner of a lot is usually shared with a neighbour. The fix is rarely one gadget. It is a route for the water, and a surface that can take the flow without eroding.

What we actually build.

Practical work aimed at where the water currently goes, not at a product catalogue.

Downspout tie-ins

Flexible line coupled to the existing downspout outlet, above grade, so the roof drains into the pipe rather than onto the ground beside it. Nothing cut into the downspout or the wall — it stays serviceable and can be pulled and flushed.

Buried drain lines

Hand-dug on the fall of the ground and run to a real outlet at the low edge of the property, so the line drains by gravity instead of sitting full of water all winter.

Rock channels & grading

River-rock runs with stone shoulders and brick edging, sized to carry the flow and hold the bed in place. Where the grade itself is the problem, we correct the surface so water leaves instead of pooling.

Our drainage process

  1. Follow the water: We walk the property in the rain if we can, or read the evidence — scour lines, settled slabs, stained fence posts, dead turf — and find where it enters, runs, and stops.
  2. Plan the route: We settle on an outlet that is genuinely lower, and a line that can reach it by gravity on your lot.
  3. Dig and tie in: Trench cut by hand, line coupled to the downspout, bed and turf opened only as wide as the work needs.
  4. Rock and restore: Channel dressed and edged, beds raked back to grade, and the site left clean the same day.
