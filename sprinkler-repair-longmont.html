What actually breaks.

Nearly every call we get is one of these four. Knowing which one it is changes the repair, the price, and whether you need us twice.

Heads & nozzles

Heads cracked by the winter, nozzles clogged with grit, and sprays that have rotated into the fence or the siding instead of the turf.

Valves & solenoids

A zone that won't open, or won't shut off. The solenoid is usually the cheap failure; the valve body is the other one.

Line breaks & leaks

A wet patch that never dries, or a zone that loses pressure. Lateral line damage usually shows up where a shovel or a root went through.

Controllers & wiring

Dead channels, cut wiring at the valve box, and controllers that lost their program after a power blink and have been watering twice a day since.

Repair first. Replace only when that's the honest answer.

A sprinkler system is a set of cheap parts on top of one buried network, and the network is usually fine. Replacing a head or a solenoid costs a fraction of a new system, and most systems in this county are worth keeping.

There are real exceptions: a main line crushed under a new driveway, or a system plumbed in a way that will keep failing every spring. When that's what we find, we'll tell you plainly and let you decide, rather than collect a repair fee every year.

How a repair call runs.

  1. Describe the symptom: which zone, what it does, and when it started. That narrows it before anyone drives out.
  2. Test the zones: we run the system and watch each zone in person, because a dead zone and a weak zone are different problems.
  3. Quote the fix: the specific part and the labor, before anything is dug up or replaced.
  4. Repair and verify: we re-run the zone, check the pressure, and show you what was actually wrong.

Sprinkler questions we get.

Only one zone won't come on. What is it?

Usually the valve, not the controller. We check the controller output, the solenoid, the wiring at the valve box, and the diaphragm in that order, because the fix and the price are very different depending on which one failed.

How much does sprinkler repair cost in Longmont?

Most calls are small: a head, a nozzle, a solenoid, or a short length of lateral line. We quote the specific repair before starting, and the on-site look is free across Longmont and Boulder County.

Can you fix a system after the first freeze?

Sometimes. The lines are already empty by then, so a valve or backflow repair is often still possible; digging in frozen ground is not. If it has to wait for spring, we say so rather than start a job we can't finish.

Do you install new sprinkler systems?

We repair, start up, and winterize systems that are already in the ground. If yours is genuinely past saving, we'll say so rather than patch it again every April.

What does a spring start-up include?

Opening the valves, walking every zone, replacing heads cracked over the winter, checking the backflow preventer for leaks, and adjusting spray so water lands on the lawn instead of the fence, the wall, or the house.
