Why fences fail here.

Almost nothing we're called back to fix is a materials problem. It's one of these:

Posts too shallow for the soil

Longmont's clay holds water in spring and shrinks hard in summer. A post set short, or in a hole that wasn't drained, works loose within a couple of seasons and takes the whole run with it.

Wind load on solid panels

A six-foot privacy fence is a sail. Posts, rails, and gate hardware have to be sized for the gusts, not for the calm week the fence went in.

Water aimed at the panels

A sprinkler head that's crept around and sprays the fence line rots the bottom rail and stains the cedar. If your system is misbehaving, that's a separate fix.

Grade and drainage at the base

A fence set into a low spot stands in water every spring. Sometimes the right answer is a short drainage fix before the posts go in, not a taller fence.

Our Longmont fencing process.

  1. Walk the line: we look at the property boundaries, the grade, the access, and what your neighbors and HOA have already built.
  2. Check the rules: height limits and setback requirements vary by zoning and by subdivision, and HOA covenants can be stricter than the city. We flag what we know before you commit to a style.
  3. Estimate the whole job: posts, footings, panels, gates, hardware, haul-off, and the turf repair along the line.
  4. Build and walk it: posts set, lines kept straight, gates hung to swing true, then a final walk-through with you.
