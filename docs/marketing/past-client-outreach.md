# Past-client outreach — fall

The cheapest revenue in the fall plan, and the only channel that needs no ad
spend, no printing, and no new page. You own the list already: every job you have
done, plus this season's case studies.

Rules that keep it from feeling like spam:

- **Text first, email second.** This trade gets read by text.
- **One message, no offer language, no links shorteners.** A name, what you're
  doing this month, and the phone number.
- **Ten a day.** More than that and replies outrun your ability to answer, which
  is worse than not sending.
- **Log every reply** against the sheet. Nothing here is automated.

---

## 1. The fall text (primary)

> Hi [Name] — it's [Your name] from Groundwork, we did [the fence / the cleanup /
> the patio] at your place in [month]. We're doing fall cleanups and sprinkler
> blowouts through November — want me to add you to the list before the slots
> fill? (720) 707-5411

Why it works: it names the job they paid for, which is the only proof of identity
they need, and it asks one small question.

## 2. The email (for anyone who prefers it, or no mobile on file)

**Subject:** Fall cleanup at [street] — want a slot?

> Hi [Name],
>
> [Your name] here from Groundwork Landscaping — we built [the cedar fence / the
> flagstone entry / the gravel bed] at your place back in [month].
>
> We're booking fall cleanups through November: leaves off the lawn and beds and
> hauled away, final mow, beds re-edged, then core aeration and overseeding in the
> same visit. Sprinkler blowouts and gutter clearing can go on the same day.
>
> Everything starts with a free walk-through, so the number you get is your yard's
> number. If you want on the list, reply here or call/text (720) 707-5411.
>
> Thanks — [Your name]
> Groundwork Landscaping · Longmont & Boulder County

## 3. The review ask (same evening as the walk-through)

> Thanks for having us out today, [Name] — glad the yard's sorted before the
> weather turns. If you've got thirty seconds, a Google review genuinely helps a
> small crew get found: [review link]. No pressure either way.

Send it while the clean yard is still in front of them, not a week later.

## 4. The referral ask (only to happy, paid clients)

> [Name] — if a neighbour is staring at the same leaf pile, send them my number.
> Two houses on the same street and I can usually do the second one cheaper,
> since I'm already there.

That is a true statement about your economics, which is why it converts.

## 5. Reactivation — quotes that went quiet

> Hi [Name], [Your name] from Groundwork — you asked about [the cleanup / the
> fence] back in [month] and we never got it scheduled. We've got room this week
> if it's still on your list. (720) 707-5411

Send once. If there's no answer, leave it — chasing twice reads as desperate and
this is a small town.

## 6. The list

One sheet, six columns, nothing fancier:

| Name | Phone | Email | Last job | Month/year | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Sort by last job descending — the most recent clients are the warmest, and this
season's case studies should be at the top of the sheet.

Mark each row: `sent`, `replied`, `booked`, `declined`, `no answer`. Two weeks in,
the booked column tells you whether the fall text or the email is the one to keep.
