Curved Block Retaining Wall, Concrete Terrace & Front Bed Rebuild

How we tore out a trampled bed against the foundation, built a curved segmental block wall on a compacted gravel base, poured a concrete terrace, and planted out the new bed — four days, one front yard.

Fort Collins, CO
Segmental Block Wall
Wall, Terrace & Planted Beds
4-Day Build

The Problem A Dead Strip Against the House

The front of this ranch had a narrow, compacted bed that had gone bare: trampled soil, weed pressure, and no grade control, so runoff from the roof and lawn washed straight across it. The bed sat on a slight fall, with nothing holding the soil where the planting was supposed to be. There was no usable outdoor space at the entry, just a concrete stoop and a mud strip.

Design & Build Step-by-Step Execution

We rebuilt the whole frontage as one system — retaining wall, terrace, and planting bed — over four working days:

  • 1. Strip & Grade: Removed the dead turf and spent bed soil, then graded the subgrade and set string lines to hold a consistent wall line and positive drainage away from the foundation.
  • 2. Compactor & Gravel Base: Compacted a classified gravel base for the wall footprint, then hand-set the first course of segmental block dead level — the step that decides whether the whole wall stays true.
  • 3. Curved Wall Build: Carried the courses around a sweeping curve, cutting blocks at the radius and stepping the cap course to follow the grade.
  • 4. Concrete Terrace: Formed and poured the concrete terrace behind the wall over a vapor barrier, screeded to fall away from the door and finished with control joints.
  • 5. Planted Bed: Backfilled the retained bed with amended soil, set boulders for structure, then planted ornamental grasses, shrubs, and perennials with a rock mulch finish.

The Result Structure, Then Planting

The bed is now held where it belongs, the entry has a real terrace instead of a stoop, and the front of the house finally has a line that reads from the street. The wall does the structural work; the planting sits on top of it instead of sliding off.
